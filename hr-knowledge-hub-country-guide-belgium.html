Salary Benchmark

  • Belgium applies automatic wage indexation (Indexation) · when prices rise by 2%, wages are adjusted accordingly · rare globally
  • National minimum monthly wage (GMMI) is set by the CAO 43 collective agreement · €2,029 in 2024/4 → ~€2,100+ in 2026
  • 13th salary (Prime de fin d’année) + double holiday pay (Pécule de vacances): mandatory in most sectors · effectively equivalent to 14 salaries
  • 2025 average full-time monthly salary ~€4,150 (gross · Statbel) · median ~€3,800 · gender pay gap continues to narrow

Belgian nominal employer ONSS is 25% · after structural reductions, the effective rate is ~22–25% · 13th salary + double holiday pay significantly increase actual cost.

Employer add-on total cost (local employee · as % of base salary)

  • ONSS 25% · 13th salary 8.33% · double holiday pay 7.67% · CCT add-ons + training
  • Employer total add-on cost is ~27 – 33% of base salary (sectors with higher CCT add-ons are closer to the upper end)

Common Benefits / Pre-tax Benefit Package

  • 13th salary + double holiday pay: almost universal · effectively 13–14 salaries · mandatory under some sector CCTs
  • Meal vouchers (Chèques-repas / Maaltijdcheques): employer subsidy €8 per working day · provided by ~80% of employers nationwide
  • Company car / bicycle / mobility allowance: standard for senior roles · from 2026, new company cars must be electric (tax incentives)
  • Group medical insurance / pension: standard in large enterprises · often required by sector CCT

Overtime Pay (Heures supplémentaires) – Working Time Act + CCT

  • Weekday overtime: +50% wage (statutory minimum) · Saturday same · CCT may provide higher rate
  • Sunday / public holiday overtime: +100% wage · mandatory minimum
  • Overtime may be converted to rest time (Récupération): 1.5× equivalent rest · subject to CCT / contract
  • Executives / trusted positions: usually contractually “no overtime counted” · applies to most Director-level roles and above

Since 2014/1, blue-collar and white-collar employment law has been unified (Statut unique) · probation period abolished · notice period increases by length of service.

Dismissal Protection and Compensation

  • Notice period (Délai de préavis): service < 3 months = 1 week · 1 year = 8 weeks · 5 years = 15 weeks · 10 years = 30 weeks
  • >10 years: +3 weeks per year (to 20 years) · then +1–2 weeks per year · employee notice period is capped at 1/2 of employer notice
  • Payment in lieu of notice (Indemnité): employer may pay wages equivalent to the notice period · includes 13th salary + holiday pay conversion
  • Collective dismissal (Loi Renault 1998): large redundancies by 20+ employee employers require prior union notification + CCT consultation

Key Regulations

Indexation Automatique — automatic wage indexation

Globally rare · when the price index (Indice santé) crosses the 2% “index threshold”, wages / social benefits / pensions are adjusted together · monitored by SCP.

Loi de 1996 / Norme salariale — wage moderation law

Sets a two-year wage growth ceiling based on wage trends in Germany / France / Netherlands · limits additional increases beyond indexation · core of national CCT negotiations.

CCT 43 / GMMI — national minimum monthly wage collective agreement

Negotiated by the National Labour Council (CNT) · sets nationwide GMMI monthly wage · sector CCTs set sector minimums above this · automatic indexation applies.

Loi sur le Bien-Être 1996 — occupational well-being law

Employers must protect physical and mental health + prevent psychosocial risks (PSR) · 50+ employers must establish CPPT well-being committee · work injury / burnout covered.

Right to Disconnect (2023) — right to disconnect law

20+ employee employers must sign a CCT or include it in work rules · no mandatory response to emails / messages outside working hours · aligned with France / Portugal.

Federal Coalition 2025 — federal coalition government reform

2025 De Wever government agreement · proposed reforms to year-end bonus / unemployment benefit duration / early retirement · some provisions gradually effective 2026–2028 (subject to legislation).
