Types of Work Visas

Immigration in Côte d'Ivoire is jointly managed by the Office National de l'Identification (ONI) under the Ministry of Interior, the Agence d'Études et de Promotion de l'Emploi (AGEPE), and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. An e-Visa system was introduced in 2017, operated by the SNEDAI Group. All ECOWAS / CEDEAO (Economic Community of West African States) member state citizens may enter visa-free and stay up to 90 days with an identity card. Below are the main work-related visa / residence permit types for non-ECOWAS nationals:

Visa / Residence Type Applicable Group Description
e-Visa Short-term business / tourism / interview candidates Apply online (snedai.com), €73, 3-month multiple entry; biometric visa sticker collected on arrival at Abidjan Félix Houphouët-Boigny International Airport (FHB); entry only via FHB airport
Visa Long Séjour (Long-stay Visa) Foreign employees employed by a Côte d'Ivoire company Entry visa for processing Carte de Séjour; visa valid for up to 90 days but allows in-country residence card application
Carte de Séjour (Residence Card) Foreigners staying in Côte d'Ivoire for more than 3 months Issued by ONI; initial validity of 1 year, renewable for 1–5 years; one card each for the employee and each accompanying family member
Permis de Travail (Work Permit) Employed foreign workers Issued by AGEPE / Ministry of Labor; valid for 1 year, renewed annually; tied to a specific employer and contract
Carte de Résident (Resident Identity Card) Foreigners with continuous legal residence in Côte d'Ivoire for 5 years or more 10-year validity; not tied to an employer; available upon application
Visa de Travail CDD / CDI Employees on fixed-term / indefinite-term employment contracts Fixed-term contract (CDD) visa up to 24 months; indefinite-term contract (CDI) visa renewed annually
ECOWAS Residence Right Citizens of the 15 ECOWAS member states Visa-free, no residence permit required to work; only need to register the employment contract with AGEPE

Marco Insight

For non-ECOWAS nationals, the standard pathway is: enter on a Visa Long Séjour, then within 90 days apply for a Carte de Séjour (ONI) + Permis de Travail (AGEPE). The following sections focus on this pathway.

Applicant Requirements

1. Employee Requirements

Valid passport (remaining validity of 6 months or more, at least 2 blank pages)
Yellow fever vaccination certificate (mandatory, internationally recognized yellow card)
Good conduct (police clearance certificate / Casier Judiciaire)
Good health (medical certificate; some consular jurisdictions require HIV and tuberculosis screening)
Academic qualifications / professional certificates (notarized in Chinese-French bilingual format and authenticated by the Côte d'Ivoire embassy abroad)
Employment contract issued by the local company (already endorsed by AGEPE)
Regulated professions (doctors, lawyers, accountants, engineers, etc.) must first register with the local professional association

2. Employer Requirements

Legally registered in Côte d'Ivoire (holding a commercial registration number RCCM and tax identification number CC / Compte Contribuable)
Enrolled in CNPS social insurance (Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance Sociale), with a valid employer registration number
Job vacancy must be advertised in a nationally circulated daily newspaper (such as Fraternité Matin, L'Inter, etc.) for at least 1 month, demonstrating that no suitable local candidates are available
Submit the advertisement and job description to AGEPE for "Visa Contrat" approval
AGEPE review: examines the employment contract + localization obligations (such as minimum local employee ratios, which vary by industry under the applicable "Convention Collective")
Commitment to provide foreign employees with housing / transportation allowances / repatriation airfare and other additional benefits (depending on the contract and industry practice)

Important Reminder

"Newspaper advertisement + AGEPE approval" is the core bottleneck of the Côte d'Ivoire work visa process: the newspaper advertisement must run for a full month before AGEPE will formally accept the application. Any missing documents or failure to meet localization requirements at any stage may result in AGEPE directly rejecting the contract (Visa Contrat refusé), requiring the entire process to restart. It is recommended to reserve newspaper advertising space as soon as recruitment begins.

Application Document Checklist

1. Employee Documents

Valid passport (remaining validity of 6 months or more) + bio-page scan
4 passport-sized photos (35×45mm, white background)
Academic certificate + degree certificate (Chinese-French / English-French bilingual notarization + authentication by Côte d'Ivoire embassy abroad)
Employment history proof (covering the past 5 years; Chinese-English / French + notarized)
Employment contract (issued by employer, endorsed by AGEPE with "Visa Contrat" annotation)
Police clearance certificate (from home country + any country of residence for 6 months or more), notarized + authenticated + translated into French
Yellow fever vaccination international certificate (International Certificate of Vaccination, yellow card)
Medical certificate / health examination report (including HIV and tuberculosis screening, issued by a Côte d'Ivoire-approved institution)
Birth certificate (notarized + authenticated + translated into French)
Marriage certificate / children's birth certificates (if accompanying family members are included)
e-Visa or Visa Long Séjour online application confirmation page + visa fee payment receipt

2. Employer Documents

Copy of company commercial registration (RCCM) + tax identification certificate (CC)
CNPS employer registration certificate (attestation CNPS)
Original newspaper advertisement (full 1-month publication period) + advertisement reference number on file
Job description (Description de poste) + recruitment justification (justification)
Employment contract (CDD or CDI), including salary, benefits, and contract duration, must be in French
AGEPE "Visa Contrat" approval form + application request letter (Demande de Visa de Contrat)
Company local-to-foreign employee ratio report (fulfilling "Convention Collective" localization requirements)
Employer guarantee letter (Lettre de Garantie), including work period + repatriation cost commitment

Note

All non-French documents must be translated into French by a Côte d'Ivoire-certified translator (traducteur assermenté) and authenticated by the Côte d'Ivoire embassy / consulate abroad. CDD (fixed-term contract) visas are valid for up to 24 months; CDI (indefinite-term contract) visas are renewed annually.

Application Process

Process Overview

Step Task Responsible Party Estimated Timeline
1 Employer reports vacancy to AGEPE + publishes recruitment advertisement in national daily newspaper Employer Approximately 1 month (advertising period)
2 After advertising period ends, employer submits employment contract for approval to AGEPE (Visa Contrat) Employer 2–4 weeks
3 After AGEPE endorses "Visa Contrat", employee applies for e-Visa or Visa Long Séjour at the Côte d'Ivoire embassy / VFS in their consular jurisdiction Employee 1–3 weeks
4 Employee enters the country with e-visa confirmation page; biometric visa sticker applied at the airport (FHB Abidjan) Employee Day of entry
5 Within 90 days of entry (maximum 10 weeks), apply to ONI for Carte de Séjour Employee 60–120 days
6 Within 3 months of starting employment, apply to AGEPE / Ministry of Labor for Permis de Travail (Work Permit) Employee / Employer 30–60 days
7 CNPS social insurance registration + tax (DGI) registration Employer Within 8 days of starting employment
8 Collect physical Carte de Séjour + Permis de Travail cards Employee 1–2 weeks after issuance

Detailed Steps

Employer registers recruitment needs in the AGEPE system (agepe.ci); simultaneously publishes recruitment advertisements in national daily newspapers such as Fraternité Matin / L'Inter / Soir Info (must run continuously for 1 month).
After the advertising period ends, the employer submits the original advertisement, job description, and candidate resume to AGEPE, applying for "Visa Contrat" (employment contract registration).
After the Visa Contrat is approved, the employee applies for an e-Visa on the SNEDAI platform (snedai.com) (€73, 3-month multiple entry) or applies for a Visa Long Séjour at the Côte d'Ivoire embassy / VFS Global center abroad (suitable for stays exceeding 3 months).
e-Visa holders must enter through Abidjan FHB International Airport, where they present their confirmation page at the airport e-Visa counter for fingerprint collection and biometric visa sticker issuance; other ports of entry do not accept e-Visas.
Begin processing the Carte de Séjour immediately after entry: submit the application to ONI (Office National de l'Identification), provide fingerprints and photos, and pay approximately XOF 200,000–500,000 (depending on the validity period).
Within 3 months of formally starting employment, apply for the Permis de Travail (Work Permit), jointly issued by AGEPE / Ministry of Labor; tied to the specific employer and position.
The employer must complete employee registration with CNPS (social insurance) + DGI (tax authority) within 8 days of the employee starting work, and include the employee in the payroll system (salary must be paid through a Côte d'Ivoire local bank account).

Note

ECOWAS citizens skip the e-Visa / Carte de Séjour process entirely and only need to register their employment contract with AGEPE upon arrival. The e-Visa is suitable for short-term business trips or interview candidates; for formal employment, it is recommended to apply directly for a Visa Long Séjour to avoid additional procedures for upgrading the visa in-country.

Post-Arrival Guide

Important Reminder

After entry, you must apply to ONI for a Carte de Séjour within 90 days (maximum 10 weeks), otherwise you will enter illegal residence status, facing daily fines (approximately XOF 5,000–25,000) or even deportation. The Permis de Travail must be obtained within 3 months of starting employment. Missing either document may result in penalties for both the employer and employee.

1. Work-Related

Within 90 days of entry: apply to ONI for Carte de Séjour (submit application, biometric collection, payment, wait for card production)
Within 3 months of starting employment: apply for Permis de Travail (AGEPE / Ministry of Labor)
CNPS social insurance registration (employer must complete within 8 days): employee contributes 6.3% of salary (pension), employer additionally contributes 7.7% pension + 5.75% family allowance + 2–5% work injury insurance (monthly salary cap: pension XOF 3,375,000; other categories XOF 70,000)
Tax registration (DGI): apply for NCC (Numéro Compte Contribuable) personal tax number; foreign employee wage tax rate is 12% (local nationals 2.8%), including ITS (wage tax) + CN (national tax)
For those intending long-term residence: after 5 consecutive years of legal residence, you may apply for a Carte de Résident (10-year resident identity card)

2. Daily Life

Open a local bank account: SGCI (Société Générale), Ecobank, UBA, NSIA, Orabank, etc.; open an account with Carte de Séjour + employment contract + proof of address
Housing: Abidjan's upscale neighborhoods are concentrated in Cocody, Marcory, and Plateau; typically requires 3 months' deposit + first month's rent; expatriates commonly find housing through Knight Frank / Coldwell Banker / Jumia House
SIM card: Orange CI, MTN CI, Moov Africa (three major carriers), registered with passport / Carte de Séjour under real-name registration
Mobile payment: Orange Money, MTN Mobile Money, Moov Money, and Wave are extremely popular in Côte d'Ivoire (essential for populations with no / limited bank access)
Transportation: Abidjan residents mainly rely on "gbaka" minibuses + "taxi compteur" + Yango / Heetch ride-hailing services; for self-driving, you need to exchange your home country driver's license for a Côte d'Ivoire license through ONSER
Healthcare: local public hospitals have limited capabilities; international health insurance is recommended (AXA / Allianz Care / Cigna, etc.); major expatriate clinics include Polyclinique Internationale Sainte Anne-Marie (PISAM), HMA, and CMI

3. Family Visas

Spouses / minor children may apply for family reunification (Regroupement Familial); the primary applicant must first hold a Carte de Séjour
Each family member must apply separately for a Carte de Séjour (separate file, separate fees)
Spouses may independently apply to AGEPE for a separate work permit (must go through the full Visa Contrat process)
Family members must provide: marriage certificate / birth certificate (notarized + French translation + Côte d'Ivoire embassy authentication), entry visa, and yellow fever yellow card
Children's schooling: French-language public schools / international schools (such as Lycée Français Jean-Mermoz, ISA, IISA, André-Malraux); places are limited, so it is recommended to contact schools before arrival

Existing Residence Permit: Changing Employers

The Côte d'Ivoire Permis de Travail is strictly tied to a specific employer and employment contract; the Carte de Séjour alone does not authorize work. Changing employers requires the new employer to complete the full AGEPE process (newspaper advertisement + Visa Contrat approval + new Permis de Travail), and the original work permit becomes void. The Carte de Séjour may be retained within its validity period, but the work permit must be reissued.

1. Core Rules

Residence / Permit Type Employer Change Rules
Permis de Travail (Work Permit) Tied to a specific employer and position; changing employers requires the new employer to restart the AGEPE process (newspaper advertisement + Visa Contrat approval + new work permit issuance)
Carte de Séjour (Residence Card) Not tied to an employer; retained within its validity period; however, a Carte de Séjour alone does not authorize legal employment and must be accompanied by a valid Permis de Travail
Carte de Résident (10-year Resident Card) Not tied to an employer or restricted by occupation; self-employment / entrepreneurship permitted; available after 5 consecutive years of legal residence
CDD (Fixed-term Contract) Visa Contract does not automatically extend upon expiry; changing employers requires signing a new CDD / CDI and reporting to AGEPE
CDI (Indefinite-term Contract) Visa Renewed annually; changing employers requires the new employer to submit a new Visa Contrat application
ECOWAS Citizens Free to move without permit restrictions, only need to register the new contract with AGEPE

2. Practical Steps for Changing Employers

Employee and former employer submit resignation / mutual termination agreement (rupture conventionnelle) under the 2015 Labor Code; employer completes separation registration with CNPS / DGI / AGEPE
Settle outstanding salary, remaining annual leave, 13th-month salary / prime de fin d'année, and severance compensation (if applicable)
Former employer cancels the original Permis de Travail with AGEPE
New employer publishes recruitment advertisement in national daily newspaper (minimum 1 month) + reports the new position to AGEPE
After the advertising period ends, the new employer submits the employment contract + candidate documents to apply for a new Visa Contrat (typically 2–4 weeks)
Employee may join the new company while the Carte de Séjour remains valid; simultaneously apply to AGEPE for a new Permis de Travail
New employer completes CNPS / DGI employment relationship registration (within 8 days)

3. Former Employer vs. New Employer: Key Comparison

Item Former Employer (Separation) New Employer (Onboarding)
AGEPE Registration Cancel original Visa Contrat + Permis de Travail New newspaper advertisement + new Visa Contrat application
CNPS Report separation + complete final month's contribution Register employee within 8 days of starting
DGI Issue salary settlement statement + separation tax settlement New contract tax registration
Contract Type Original CDD / CDI terminated New CDD (up to 24 months) / CDI
Severance Compensation Calculated per Article 18-11 of the Labor Code (tiered by years of service) N/A
Separation Documents Issue work certificate (certificat de travail) + final pay statement (solde de tout compte) New job description + new contract

Important Reminder

During the employer change period, the employee's original Permis de Travail is void, and the employee must not begin working for the new employer until the new permit is issued. Otherwise, it is considered illegal employment (travail illégal), and both employer and employee face fines of XOF 500,000 to XOF 5,000,000. It is recommended to initiate the job change process while the Carte de Séjour has more than 6 months of remaining validity, and to require the new employer to complete the Visa Contrat approval before the employee formally starts work.

4. Permanent Residence Pathway

After 5 consecutive years of legal residence (continuously holding a Carte de Séjour with no major legal violations), you may apply to ONI for a Carte de Résident (10-year resident identity card). This card is not tied to an employer or restricted by field of employment, permits self-employment and entrepreneurship, and allows extended absences from the country (up to 6 months) without losing residence status. The approval process focuses on: continuous legal tax payment (DGI), complete CNPS contribution records, and criminal record history.

Departure Guide

Submit resignation notice (préavis, ranging from 8 days to 4 months depending on years of service) under the 2015 Labor Code; employer completes "solde de tout compte" (final pay settlement)
Settle remaining annual leave (minimum 2.5 days/month per year) + 13th-month salary (prime de fin d'année) + severance / retirement compensation (tiered by years of service)
Employer cancels Permis de Travail with AGEPE / Ministry of Labor; reports employee separation to CNPS; obtains final wage tax settlement from DGI (attestation de régularité fiscale)
Cancel Carte de Séjour with ONI (if no longer residing) or apply for suspension
CNPS contribution records: non-ECOWAS departing employees generally cannot receive refunds; if the home country has a bilateral social security agreement with Côte d'Ivoire (including China, though the agreement scope is limited), contribution years may be accumulated toward the home country's system
Close local bank account: it is recommended to keep the account open for 1–2 months for salary settlement and final utility bills; before closing, consider retaining a digital bank account with NSIA / SGCI for potential future return to Côte d'Ivoire
Cancel phone / internet contracts; close Orange Money / MTN MoMo and other mobile payment accounts; return Carte de Séjour (required by some ONI branch offices)
Personal income tax settlement: apply to DGI for a "Quitus Fiscal" (tax clearance certificate), proving no outstanding taxes, which facilitates departure and future re-entry applications
If returning to Côte d'Ivoire shortly after departure: simply retain a valid Carte de Séjour (maximum permitted absence of 6 months); exceeding this period is considered abandonment of residence status

Key Policy Changes 2015–2026

Since the enactment of the new Labor Code in 2015, Côte d'Ivoire has continued to advance the digitalization and localization balance of employment and immigration management. Below are the key changes in recent years:

Change Details
New Labor Code Effective (2015) Loi n° 2015-532 du 20 juillet 2015 replaced the previous law; clarified severance compensation calculation formulas, probation period caps, and contract termination procedures; foreign employees receive equal labor protections as local employees
e-Visa System Launch (2017) Operated by SNEDAI Group, online application at €73, 3-month multiple entry; biometric visa sticker collected only at Abidjan FHB airport upon entry
AGEPE Digitalization (2018–2020) "Visa Contrat" applications, recruitment advertisement filing, and Permis de Travail applications gradually moved online (agepe.ci)
ECOWAS Free Movement Agreement Strengthened Citizens of the 15 West African nations are visa-free + exempt from Carte de Séjour to work; ECOWAS single identity card pilot launched in 2024 (covering select countries)
Carte de Résident (10-year) Established a 10-year resident identity card for long-term foreign residents; not tied to an employer, facilitating entrepreneurship and cross-industry mobility
VFS Global Partnership (2022) Visa applications from certain countries (including China) are processed by VFS Global, shortening consulate processing times
Foreign Employee Tax Rate Maintained at 12% ITS (wage tax) for foreign employees at 12% vs. 2.8% for local nationals; unchanged despite multiple international investment facilitation policies ("2030 National Strategy")
Yellow Fever Certificate Digitalization Starting in 2024, some consulates accept ICAO-standard electronic yellow cards
CNPS Monthly Salary Cap Adjustment (2023) Pension contribution base cap raised to XOF 3,375,000; other insurance category cap at XOF 70,000
Intensified Crackdown on Illegal Employment (2025) Joint enforcement upgraded by AGEPE / Immigration Bureau; maximum single fine of XOF 5,000,000 for employers hiring workers without Permis de Travail
