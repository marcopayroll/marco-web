Scope & Application

Applicable Scope and Legal Basis / Lag om anställningsskydd · Semesterlag · Inkomstskattelag

This document applies to:

Companies that have established a local legal entity in Sweden (Aktiebolag AB / Handelsbolag / Kommanditbolag), registered with Bolagsverket
Employees directly employed by the local entity, subject to the Employment Protection Act (LAS) and applicable collective agreements (kollektivavtal)
Payroll compliance based on LAS, the Annual Leave Act (Semesterlag), the Income Tax Act (Inkomstskattelag), and regulations from Skatteverket / Försäkringskassan

MARCO INSIGHT

Three key characteristics of Sweden’s Payroll: (1) There is no statutory minimum wage; minimum pay is set by industry-level kollektivavtal (collective agreements). (2) There is no social contribution cap: arbetsgivaravgifter at 31.42% applies to every krona of salary, bonus, and taxable benefits. (3) Income tax is primarily municipal tax (approximately 32%), with only high earners (>SEK 643,000/year) paying an additional 20% state tax.

MARCO INSIGHT

Three commonly underestimated details in Sweden’s Payroll: (1) Employer social contributions at 31.42% have no contribution cap whatsoever: even million-krona salaries are subject to the full rate, making it the largest cost item for employers of high earners. (2) Similarly, there is no social contribution income cap, but the dual relief from Jobbskatteavdrag + Grundavdrag means an employee earning SEK 50,000/month has an effective tax rate of approximately 26-28%, far below the “headline rate” of 32%+20%. (3) Kollektivavtal (industry collective agreements) function as de facto mandatory obligations: companies that have signed an agreement must additionally pay ITP (white-collar approximately 4.5%-30%) and AGS/TGL (approximately 1%), pushing actual employer costs above 36%.

MARCO INSIGHT

Three key changes for Sweden Payroll in 2026: (1) SINK non-resident special tax reduced from 25% to 22.5% (further reduction to 20% in 2027), applicable to cross-border employees working in Sweden but permanently residing abroad. (2) Expertskatt proposal effective 2026 increases the tax-free proportion from 25% to 30%, with simplified eligibility (R&D doctoral candidates may be exempt from the salary threshold). (3) From 1 January 2026, employers of 67+ employees pay only the 10.21% pension portion (previously the full 31.42%); from April 2026, a youth employment reduction for 19-23-year-olds takes effect through September 2027.
