Types of Work Visas

Malaysia immigration matters are administered by Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia (JIM). Expatriate professionals are mainly processed through Expatriate Services Division (ESD / MyXpats), while manufacturing and selected services are processed through the MIDA Expatriate System (MES, launched from March 2026). The main foreign-employee visa is the Employment Pass (EP) I / II / III. From 1 June 2026, new salary thresholds and mandatory JTKSM labor approval apply.

Visa TypeWho It Applies ToDescription (2026)
Employment Pass I (EP I)C-Level executives, directors, and key-post professionalsMonthly salary >= RM 20,000. Contract term 1–5 years. Eligible to bring spouse / children on Dependent Pass and may apply for a domestic helper. Not tied to minor position changes.
Employment Pass II (EP II)Mid-to-senior professionals, managers, engineersMonthly salary RM 10,000–19,999. Contract term up to 2 years. Dependent Pass available. Employer must submit a Succession Plan.
Employment Pass III (EP III)Technical staff and junior professionalsMonthly salary RM 5,000–9,999; manufacturing and manufacturing services threshold RM 7,000–9,999. Contract up to 12 months. No Dependent Pass. Maximum 2 renewals. Succession Plan required.
Professional Visit Pass (PVP)Short-term assignees from overseas companies serving a Malaysian client / projectSalary remains paid by the overseas employer. Maximum 12 months. Cannot renew beyond 12 months. No dependents. Used for audit, training, deployment, and project work.
Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T)High-end talent already holding EP for at least 3 years and earning at least RM 15,000 / monthReviewed by TalentCorp. 10-year pass. Not employer-bound; holder may change jobs or start a business. Spouse may work; children may study in Malaysia.
MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home)High-net-worth individuals seeking long-term residence, not a work visaReformed in 2024 into Silver / Gold / Platinum tiers. Minimum fixed deposit USD 150,000 and minimum property purchase RM 600,000. Holders cannot be employed in Malaysia, but may start a business.
Premium Visa Programme (PVIP)High-net-worth long-term residence applicants20-year renewable long-term residence. Overseas monthly income >= RM 40,000 and fixed deposit RM 1,000,000. Holder may work, start a business, invest, and study in Malaysia.

Employment Pass as the Mainstream Route

Employment Pass is the mainstream Malaysian work-visa route for foreign employees and covers more than 90% of work-related applications. The sections below focus on EP I / II / III while also covering PVP and RP-T.

Applicant Requirements

1. Employee-Side Requirements

(1) Employment Pass I (EP I)

Salary threshold: >= RM 20,000 per month, referring only to fixed basic salary in the contract, excluding allowances, commission, and bonus.
Education: bachelor’s degree or equivalent professional qualification, matched to the role.
Experience: at least 3 years of relevant industry experience; director / senior executive roles usually require 10+ years.
Role must be a Key Post, such as CEO, CFO, CTO, Managing Director, etc.
No strict age cap; director posts generally require age >= 27, other posts usually >= 23.

(2) Employment Pass II (EP II)

Monthly salary threshold: RM 10,000–19,999, fixed basic salary only.
Education: bachelor’s degree or above relevant to the role; or diploma plus at least 5 years of relevant experience.
Experience: at least 3 years.
Age: at least 23.
Employer must submit a Succession Plan showing local replacement within 2–3 years.

(3) Employment Pass III (EP III)

Monthly salary threshold: RM 5,000–9,999; manufacturing and manufacturing services sub-threshold RM 7,000–9,999.
Education: diploma plus related training certificate, or fresh bachelor’s graduate.
Experience: at least 2 years.
Maximum contract term: 12 months; maximum 2 renewals, meaning up to 3 years total.
No Dependent Pass or domestic-helper sponsorship.

(4) Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T)

Already working in Malaysia continuously on EP for at least 3 years.
Monthly basic salary >= RM 15,000; annual salary >= RM 180,000.
Has a Malaysian Tax File Number and has filed taxes for 2 years.
Bachelor’s / master’s / doctoral degree or professional qualification, plus at least 5 years of total work experience.
EP should have more than 3 months validity remaining at the time of application.

2. Employer-Side Requirements

Employer must be a legally registered company with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM).
The company must first register through ESD, or through MIDA-MES for manufacturing, and obtain Expatriate Posts Approval.
Paid-up capital: Malaysian-owned >= RM 250,000; 50%–99% foreign-owned >= RM 350,000; 100% foreign-owned >= RM 500,000. Some sectors require more, for example construction RM 1,500,000.
From 1 June 2026: all EP I / II / III applications must first obtain JTKSM Section 60K approval, taking about 2–3 weeks.
For EP II / III: the role must first be advertised on MyFutureJobs for 30 days to prove insufficient local talent.
For EP II / III: a written Succession Plan is required, including local successor, training plan, and timeline.
From Oct. 2025: foreign employees must contribute to EPF (employer 2%, employee 2%) and SOCSO employment-injury coverage (employer 1.25%).

Important Reminder

From 1 June 2026, Malaysia EP policy tightens significantly. EP I doubles from RM 10,000 to RM 20,000; EP II rises from RM 5,000 to RM 10,000; JTKSM Section 60K approval becomes mandatory; EP II / III require a local succession plan. New rules apply to all applications and renewals submitted after 1 June 2026.

Application Materials Checklist

1. Employee Documents

Valid passport with at least 18 months remaining and at least 2 blank pages + scan of all pages, including blank pages.
Passport photo, 35×45 mm, white background, taken within the last 3 months.
Academic certificates and transcript, verified by China education authorities / WES or similar; notarized translation required if not in English.
Detailed CV / resume covering all work experience, dates, and responsibilities.
Employment contract with salary details, position, start date, contract term, and employer signature.
Job Description Letter issued by the employer.
Reference Letters from previous employers.
Professional certificates such as ACCA, CFA, PMP, etc., if applicable.
Criminal-record certificate for certain sectors / senior executive posts.
Marriage certificate / children’s birth certificates for Dependent Pass applications, with Apostille / notarized translation.

2. Employer Documents

ESD company registration number activated, or MIDA-MES registration for manufacturing.
SSM corporate documents such as Form 9 / Form 24 / Form 49 or Superform + latest organisation chart.
Latest audited financial statements including balance sheet and profit-and-loss statement.
Company profile, business scope, local employee count, and foreign employee count.
JTKSM Section 60K approval letter, mandatory from 1 June 2026.
MyFutureJobs advertisement screenshots and statistics for EP II / III, mandatory for 30 days.
Succession Plan for EP II / III.
Expatriate Posts Approval letter.
Fees: EP processing fee RM 200 / year + Pass Sticker fee RM 500 + visa fee by nationality, e.g. China RM 75.

Note: Education Verification

Education verification is a common bottleneck for Chinese applicants. Prepare degree / graduation verification, Apostille, and English translation early; the process usually takes about 3–4 weeks.

Detailed Steps

1.Company registration: employer submits SSM documents, audit report, and company profile in ESD to activate Sponsoring Company status. Manufacturing and MIDA-regulated industries use MES from March 2026.
2.Local recruitment proof: EP II / III roles must be advertised on MyFutureJobs for 30 days. Employers should conduct genuine local interviews and keep records for JTKSM review. EP I is generally exempt.
3.JTKSM Section 60K approval: mandatory from 1 June 2026. Employer proves insufficient local candidates and provides a Succession Plan for EP II / III. Processing takes about 2–3 weeks.
4.Expatriate Posts Approval: employer applies through ESD or MES for new / renewed expatriate post quota. JTKSM approval is a prerequisite document.
5.EP online application: employer completes employee details, uploads passport, education, contract, Job Description, and Succession Plan where required, then pays the fee.
6.LoA issuance: ESD / MIDA normally reviews within 5–10 business days and issues the Letter of Approval. LoA is valid for 6 months.
7.Entry visa: mainland Chinese passport holders use the LoA to apply for Visa with Reference (VDR) or Single Entry Visa (SEV) through the Malaysian mission / VFS, normally 3–7 business days.
8.Entry and sticker: employee enters with LoA + VDR and completes Pass Sticker endorsement and biometrics at the designated ESD center within 30 days.
9.Renewal: renewal is similar to a new application. Start about 3 months before expiry; after 1 June 2026, all renewals must meet the new salary thresholds.

Parallel Expatriate Systems

Malaysia has parallel expatriate systems: MIDA-regulated manufacturing and high-tech services use MIDA Expatriate System (MES); other sectors such as finance, trade, IT, retail, and F&B use ESD MyXpats. MDEC-regulated ICT / digital-economy companies may also have a dedicated Knowledge Worker route.

Landing Guide

Important Reminder: 30-Day Pass Sticker Deadline

After entering with a valid Letter of Approval (LoA) and VDR / SEV, the employee must complete Pass Sticker endorsement and biometrics at an ESD service center within 30 days. The Pass Sticker in the passport is the main proof of legal work and residence.

1. Work-Related Matters

Within 30 days after entry: complete Pass Sticker endorsement at an ESD service center such as Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya, Penang, Johor Bahru, or Kuching.
Apply for personal tax number (Tax File Number / Nombor Cukai Pendapatan) through LHDN or via employer HR, usually within 2 weeks.
EPF registration: from Oct. 2025 foreign employees must join EPF, with employer 2% and employee 2%. Funds may be withdrawn on departure.
SOCSO employment-injury coverage: employer contributes 1.25% of basic salary, capped at RM 6,000 wage base. EIS does not apply to foreign employees.
Income tax: non-residents (< 182 days) are withheld at a flat 30%; tax residents (>= 182 days) are taxed progressively from 0%–30%. Employer deducts monthly PCB.
After 3 years on EP and salary >= RM 15,000, eligible talent may apply for RP-T for 10-year free employment status.

2. Daily-Life Matters

Commercial medical insurance is recommended because public healthcare charges for foreign employees can be high. Common insurers include AIA, Allianz, and Great Eastern.
Bank accounts: Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank, Hong Leong, HSBC; normally EP Pass + employer letter + passport are required.
Housing: PropertyGuru, iProperty, Mudah.my. Popular expat areas include KLCC, Mont Kiara, and Bangsar. Deposit usually 2.5 months (2 months rent + 0.5 month utilities).
Mobile: Maxis / Hotlink, Celcom, Digi, U Mobile. Prepaid needs passport + EP; postpaid plans are commonly RM 80–150 per month.
Transport: Kuala Lumpur LRT / MRT / Monorail; Touch n Go card is widely used. Grab is the main ride-hailing and delivery app. Foreign drivers should use China licence + IDP for the first 90 days, then switch to a JPJ licence if needed.
Mobile payment: Touch n Go eWallet, GrabPay, Boost, ShopeePay. Visa / Mastercard / UnionPay acceptance is high; WeChat Pay / Alipay may work in Chinese business areas.

3. Family Visas

Dependent Pass (DP): available only to EP I / EP II holders for legal spouse and unmarried children under 18. Validity follows the main EP.
DP holders cannot work in Malaysia. To work, they must switch to their own EP or PVP.
Long Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP): for family members not eligible for DP, such as parents, parents-in-law, unmarried partner, or adult children. Up to 5 years.
LTSVP holders generally cannot work, except Malaysian citizen spouses or cases with an Endorsement to Work.
DP documents: marriage / birth certificate with Apostille notarization + English translation, principal EP Pass, proof of address, and dependents’ passports.
Children may attend international or private schools. Public-school admission requires special approval. EP I / II children have no general study restriction.
Domestic helper: only EP I holders with monthly salary >= RM 20,000 may apply for one Maid Pass.

Existing Visa: Changing Employer

Malaysia Employment Pass is strictly bound to the specific employer. When changing employer, the old EP must be cancelled first and the new employer must submit a fresh EP application. The pass cannot be directly transferred or renewed under the new employer. Only RP-T holders may change employers freely.

1. Core Rules

Visa TypeRule for Changing Employer
Employment Pass I / II / IIIStrictly employer-bound. Existing EP must be cancelled first, then the new employer files a fresh EP application through JTKSM + ESD. Processing can take 4–8 weeks; from 1 June 2026 the new salary thresholds apply.
Professional Visit Pass (PVP)Bound to the overseas sending company. Cannot change to a Malaysian employer; must apply for EP if locally employed.
Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T)Fully flexible. Holder may work for any Malaysian company, start a business, or change employers without a new EP; only notify TalentCorp of the new employer.
Dependent PassFollows the principal EP. When the principal changes employer, DP ends and must be re-applied with the new EP.
MM2H / PVIPMM2H holders generally cannot be employed by Malaysian companies. PVIP holders may work / start a business freely; employer-change issue generally does not arise.

2. Practical Steps for Changing Employers

1.Employee signs the Offer Letter with the new employer and confirms that the employer is ESD-registered, has expatriate post quota, and meets the 2026 salary threshold.
2.Original employer initiates EP Cancellation on ESD. Employee returns the old Pass Sticker. ESD issues a Cancellation Letter, usually in 3–5 business days.
3.New employer applies for JTKSM Section 60K approval if not already obtained. EP II / III also require 30-day MyFutureJobs advertisement and Succession Plan.
4.New employer submits a fresh EP application on ESD / MES with passport, new contract, old EP Cancellation Letter, and new employer documents.
5.ESD reviews within 5–10 business days and issues a new LoA. If the employee is in Malaysia, LoA can be used for new endorsement; if outside Malaysia, the employee applies for a new VDR.
6.Special Pass buffer: after EP cancellation, the employee may apply for a 30-day Special Pass to maintain legal stay, renewable up to 2 times, max 90 days.
7.Dependent Pass family members should be re-applied with the new EP; ideally immediately after the principal EP is approved.

Important Reminder: Status Gap Risk

Changing employers creates a risk of status gap. From EP cancellation to new EP issuance, the employee cannot legally work. If there is a gap, apply for Special Pass (30 days each, up to 90 days). If no new EP is issued after 90 days, the employee must leave Malaysia.

4. RP-T: Key Route Out of Employer Binding

Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T) is a 10-year flexible pass for high-end expatriate talent, reviewed by TalentCorp. It is not tied to an employer. RP-T holders may work for any Malaysian company, be self-employed, start a business, and sponsor family members for the same long-term period. Core eligibility: 3 years on EP, monthly salary >= RM 15,000, and 2 years of Malaysian tax filing. For senior talent planning long-term Malaysia employment, RP-T is the strongest route to escape employer binding.

Departure Guide

1.Submit resignation according to the employment contract, usually 1–3 months depending on service period, and agree final working date and salary settlement.
2.If dismissal falls under Section 13A retrenchment, severance is generally calculated by service: 1–2 years = 10 days/year; 2–5 years = 15 days/year; over 5 years = 20 days/year.
3.Employer initiates EP Cancellation in ESD and returns the Pass Sticker. ESD issues Cancellation Letter; employee is usually required to depart within 30 days.
4.LHDN tax clearance: employer must notify LHDN 30 days before final work date and withhold final tax. Without Tax Clearance Letter, employer may have to freeze the last 2 months’ salary.
5.EPF withdrawal: foreign employees may withdraw EPF in full with proof of departure, passport, and Tax Clearance. Processing usually takes 4–6 weeks.
6.SOCSO termination: employer reports departure to PERKESO; the employee will not receive future SOCSO benefits.
7.Dependent Pass / LTSVP family passes are cancelled together with the principal EP.
8.Close Malaysian bank accounts after allowing 1–2 months for final utilities, internet, insurance, and salary settlement.
9.Cancel commercial insurance and phone / internet contracts. Telecom early termination may cost about RM 200–500.
10.Keep the LHDN Tax File Number if future return to Malaysia is possible; it can be reused for future employment.

Key Policy Changes for 2024–2026

Malaysia has recently balanced attracting high-end foreign talent with protecting local employment and RMK-13 priorities. Key changes from 2024 to 2026 are below:

Policy ChangeContent
EP salary thresholds increased sharply (2026-06-01)EP I: RM 10,000 → RM 20,000; EP II: RM 5,000 → RM 10,000; EP III: RM 3,000 → RM 5,000, with manufacturing RM 7,000. Applies to new applications and renewals after 1 June 2026.
JTKSM Section 60K pre-approval (2026-06-01)All EP I / II / III applications require labor-department approval first. EP II / III must advertise on MyFutureJobs for 30 days.
Succession Plan mandatory (2026-06-01)EP II / III employers must submit a written local succession plan with successor, training, and 2–3 year timeline.
EPF mandatory for foreign employees (2025-10)Foreign employees join EPF for the first time. Employer and employee each contribute 2%. Full withdrawal possible on departure.
MIDA Expatriate System launched (2026-03)MIDA-regulated industries, including manufacturing and some high-tech services, process EP / PVP / DP through MES. Other sectors continue with ESD MyXpats.
SOCSO wage base increased (2024-10)SOCSO + EIS salary ceiling increased from RM 4,000 to RM 6,000, raising employer cost. EIS still excludes foreign workers.
MM2H three-tier reform (2024-06)Silver / Gold / Platinum tiers introduced with fixed deposit USD 150K–1M and mandatory property purchase RM 600K–2M. Minimum age 25.
PVIP long-residence route improved (2024–2026)20-year renewable residence; overseas monthly income >= RM 40,000 and fixed deposit RM 1,000,000. Holder may work, invest, study, or start a business.
RP-T threshold retained (2024)RP-T remains at salary >= RM 15,000 and at least 3 years on EP. TalentCorp remains the reviewing body; 10-year pass with free job change.
China Apostille simplification (2023-11)After China joined the Hague Apostille Convention, Chinese public documents such as birth, marriage, and academic documents can be used in Malaysia with Apostille, without double legalization.
