Types of Work Visas

Employer-sponsored visas in Australia are managed by the Department of Home Affairs. Since late 2024, the former TSS (482) visa has been replaced by the Skills in Demand (SID) framework. The main employer-sponsored visa types are:

Visa CategoryWho It Applies ToDescription
Subclass 482 Skills in DemandSkilled workers / professionalsMain temporary employer-sponsored visa; valid for up to 4 years; includes Core Skills, Specialist Skills, and Labour Agreement streams; can lead to 186 permanent residence.
Subclass 186 ENSEmployees already holding 482 / direct applicantsPermanent residence visa; includes TRT (after 2 years of work on 482), Direct Entry, and Labour Agreement streams.
Subclass 494Regional employer sponsorshipTemporary visa; valid for 5 years; limited to regional areas outside Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane; may lead to 191 PR after 3 years.
Subclass 407Trainees / internsFor foreign nationals receiving occupational training in Australia; valid for up to 2 years.

Applicant Requirements (Subclass 482)

Employee Side

Has qualifications and work experience relevant to the nominated occupation (at least 1 year of full-time relevant experience).
Meets English requirement: IELTS at least 5.0 in each band (or equivalent; at least 6.0 is required when transferring to 186 PR).
Meets salary threshold: Core Skills stream at least AUD 73,150/year; Specialist Skills stream at least AUD 141,210/year.
Occupation is on the CSOL for the Core Skills stream, or has no occupation-list restriction under the Specialist Skills stream.
No upper age limit for 482; generally must be under 45 when transferring to 186.
Passes health and character checks.
Holds a valid passport.

Employer Side

Approved as a Standard Business Sponsor (SBS).
Submits the position nomination to the Department of Home Affairs.
Proves that no suitable Australian local candidate can be found (Labour Market Testing).
Pays the SAF Levy: AUD 1,200/year for businesses with annual turnover under AUD 10 million; AUD 1,800/year for businesses with annual turnover of AUD 10 million or more.
Contract salary must be no lower than the CSIT/SSIT or the market annual salary, whichever is higher.
Employer must not pass the SAF Levy or nomination fee on to the employee.

Application Document Checklist

Employee Documents

Valid passport and scanned personal information page.
English test result (IELTS / PTE / TOEFL, etc.).
Academic certificates and professional qualification certificates.
Skills assessment report (required for some occupations).
Work experience proof / reference letters (at least 1 year of full-time relevant experience).
Police clearance certificate (AFP or police authority in the country of origin).
Health examination report from an approved medical provider.
Resume / CV in English.
Passport-sized photo.

Employer Documents

SBS approval letter (Standard Business Sponsorship).
Nomination Form.
Labor Market Testing evidence (recruitment advertisement records, etc.).
Company financial statements / business registration documents.
Proof of SAF Levy payment.

Application Process

Process Overview

Step Action Responsible Party Estimated Time

2 Employer submits the position nomination to Home Affairs. Employer Approx. 1–4 months
3 Employee submits the visa application online through ImmiAccount. Employee —
4 Complete health examination and character checks. Employee Approx. 2–4 weeks
5 Wait for visa decision. Home Affairs Overall approx. 2–6 months

Note

Employers are advised to start the process at least 6 months before the planned overseas hire. SBS, nomination, and visa steps may be prepared in parallel, but the overall timeline is still usually 2–6 months.

Arrival Guide

Important Reminder

A 482 visa holder may only work for the nominated employer. Changing employers without approval may lead to visa cancellation.

Work-Related

Obtain a Tax File Number (TFN).
Set up Superannuation (retirement account). Employer contribution is mandatory at 11.5%.
Register for Medicare if the country of origin has a reciprocal agreement with Australia, or purchase private health insurance.
Provide the TFN and Super account information to the employer.

Daily Life

Open a local bank account (CommBank / ANZ / NAB / Westpac).
Find housing, preferably through Domain / REA / Flatmates and similar platforms.
Set up a local SIM card (Telstra / Optus / Vodafone).
Apply for a public transport card such as Opal Card / Myki / Go Card.
Convert or apply for a driver licence, subject to state rules.

Dependent Visas

482 holders may apply for dependent visas for their spouse / de facto partner and unmarried children under 23.
The spouse / partner as a secondary applicant has full work rights with no employer restriction.
Dependent children may attend public school in Australia.

Existing Visa: Changing Employers

Australia's 482/SID visa is employer-sponsored and linked to a specific employer. To change employers, the new employer must complete the sponsorship, nomination, and visa application process again.

Core Rules

Item Rule
Relationship between visa and employer The 482 visa is linked to the nominated employer. The employee may only work for that employer.
After employment ends There is a 60-day continuous grace period, or up to 180 cumulative days during the visa period, to find a new employer.
Employer change process New employer must complete: SBS sponsorship → new position nomination → employee submits a new visa application.
PR time accumulation Time worked on 482 can be accumulated across different sponsors; 2 years total is required to transfer to 186 TRT.
After 186 PR approval After receiving 186 permanent residence, the employee may change employers freely without approval.

From April 2020, employers must provide a Written Statement of Particulars on the first day of employment

Practical Steps to Change Employers

Terminate the employment relationship with the original employer; the 60-day grace period starts automatically.
Find a new employer and confirm that it already has, or is willing to apply for, SBS sponsorship.
The new employer submits a new position nomination to Home Affairs.
The employee submits a new 482 visa application through ImmiAccount.
Wait for the new visa decision. During this period, the employee may remain and work lawfully on a Bridging Visa where applicable.

Note

If no new employer is found within the 60-day grace period, the visa may be cancelled and the employee must leave Australia. It is recommended to discuss sponsorship interest with new employers before resignation.

Comparison of Employer Change Rules by Visa Status

Visa Type Employer Change Rule PR Impact
482 / SID New employer must complete SBS + nomination + new visa application; 60-day grace period applies. PR time may be accumulated across employers.
494 Regional Same as above, but the new employer must also be located in a regional area. Changing employers may affect 191 PR eligibility.
186 Permanent Residence Free to change employers; no approval required. Not affected.

Since November 2023, the work period required to transfer from 482 to 186 TRT has been reduced from 3 years to 2 years, and the time may be accumulated across employers. This significantly improves employee mobility.

Departure Guide

Employee Departure Checklist

Terminate the employment contract according to the notice period required under Australia's Fair Work Act.
Employer notifies Home Affairs that the employment relationship has ended.
Employee has 60 days to find a new employer; otherwise, they must leave Australia.
Complete tax clearance / tax return with the ATO.
Close bank accounts.
Cancel health insurance / Medicare where applicable.
Superannuation may be withdrawn after permanent departure through DASP (Departing Australia Superannuation Payment).

Key Policy Changes from 2024 to 2026

Change Item Details
SID replaces TSS The Skills in Demand framework formally replaced the previous TSS (482) structure and simplified it into Core, Specialist, and Labour Agreement streams.
CSOL occupation list The new Core Skills Occupation List combines the previous MLTSSL, STSOL, and ROL lists into one list with 456 occupations.
Salary thresholds increased CSIT increased from AUD 70,000 to AUD 73,150 (2025–26); SSIT increased to AUD 141,210. Thresholds are indexed every 1 July.
PR pathway shortened The 482 to 186 TRT work requirement was reduced from 3 years to 2 years, and time may be accumulated across employers.
Work experience reduced The requirement was reduced from the previous 2 years to 1 year of full-time relevant experience, giving Australia-based graduates earlier access to sponsorship opportunities.
Employer change rules relaxed After leaving employment, there is a 60-day grace period (previously 28 days → 60 days); cumulative time during the visa period may reach 180 days.
Anti-exploitation legislation The Migration Amendment (Combatting Migrant Exploitation) Act 2026 strengthens penalties against employers who exploit migrant workers and protects visa holders' rights.
