Scope & Application

Applicable Scope and Legal Basis / Labor Standards Act · 4 Major Insurances Act · Income Tax Act

This document applies to:

Companies that have established a local legal entity in South Korea (주식회사 Chusik Hoesa / 유한회사 Yuhan Hoesa), with commercial registration completed at the district court
Employees directly employed by the local entity, subject to the Labor Standards Act (Geunro Gijunbeop)
Payroll compliance based on the Income Tax Act, the 4 Major Insurances Acts, and regulations from the National Tax Service (NTS) / National Pension Service / National Health Insurance Service

MARCO INSIGHT

South Korea’s Payroll is centred on the 4 Major Social Insurances (4 Daeboheom): National Pension (NP) + National Health Insurance (NHI) + Employment Insurance (EI) + Workers’ Compensation (WC), shared between employer and employee. The Year-End Tax Adjustment (Yeonmal Jeongsan) is a uniquely Korean tax event: employers must perform a full-year PIT reconciliation for each employee in February of the following year, with refunds or additional collections. This process is operationally intensive.

MARCO INSIGHT

Three commonly underestimated details in South Korea’s Payroll: (1) Severance Pay (Toejikgeum) is a statutory additional cost: for employees with 1+ year of service, employers must pay 1 month’s average salary × years of service, settled within 14 days. It is recommended to pre-provision or participate in a retirement pension (DC/DB) scheme (퇴직연금) to manage this liability. (2) National Pension monthly contribution base is capped at KRW 6,370,000; contributions stop increasing once the cap is reached. However, health insurance is calculated independently with a cap of KRW 10,390,220. (3) Year-End Tax Adjustment (Yeonmal Jeongsan) is performed every February for all employees’ PIT reconciliation, factoring in deductions for credit card spending, medical expenses, education, insurance, etc., with refunds or additional collections applied.

MARCO INSIGHT

Three key changes for South Korea Payroll in 2026: (1) Minimum hourly wage increased by 1.7% to KRW 10,320, with a monthly salary threshold of KRW 2,156,880. (2) Paternity leave extended from 10 days to 20 days (from February 2026), fully paid by Employment Insurance. (3) From October 2025, the 20% annual interest penalty for late salary payment has been expanded from “former employees only” to include “current employees”. HR must strictly ensure payroll is processed on time.
