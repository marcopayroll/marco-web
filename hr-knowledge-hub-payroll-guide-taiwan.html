Scope & Application

This document applies to:

Companies that have established a local legal entity in Taiwan (Company Limited by Shares / Limited Company)
Employees directly employed by the local entity, subject to the Labour Standards Act and related labour regulations
Payroll compliance based on the Income Tax Act, Labour Insurance Act, National Health Insurance Act, and Labour Pension Act

This framework explains: how companies complete Payroll Set-up, salary calculation, monthly filing and payment, and annual compliance management in Taiwan.

MARCO INSIGHT

Taiwan’s Payroll is centred on the “Three Insurances and One Fund” (Labour Insurance + National Health Insurance + Employment Insurance + Labour Pension). The Labour Pension New System (6% employer contribution) operates as a defined contribution scheme and is one of the largest statutory labour costs for employers. Additionally, Taiwan’s income tax follows an “annual filing system”: employers withhold tax monthly based on the withholding tax tables, but employees ultimately file their own consolidated income tax return in May of the following year.

MARCO INSIGHT

Taiwan’s special leave system can be calculated based on either the “date of employment anniversary” method or the “calendar year” method. Both methods are legally valid but have different calculation logic. The anniversary method uses the employee’s hire date anniversary as the benchmark, while the calendar year method uses 1 January as the cycle and prorates accordingly. Most companies choose the calendar year method for unified management, but must pay attention to prorated calculations for the first and last years to avoid under-granting entitlements.

Key Rules:

Both Labour Insurance and NHI use “Insured Salary/Amount Bracket Tables” to determine the applicable bracket, rather than directly multiplying the actual salary by the rate
Employee NHI premiums must include dependant count: each additional dependant increases the employee’s self-paid premium by 1 times (maximum 3 dependants)
Second-generation NHI supplementary premium: when a single bonus exceeds 4 times the monthly insured amount, 2.11% must be withheld on the excess

MARCO INSIGHT

NHI dependant surcharge is a unique mechanism in Taiwan’s Payroll. If an employee’s spouse and lineal relatives have no other insurance coverage, they must be enrolled under the employee’s NHI, with the premium borne by the employee. NHI premium = insured amount × 5.17% × 30% × (1 + number of dependants), with a maximum of 3 dependants counted. The Payroll system must maintain each employee’s dependant count and calculate dynamically.

v. Withholding Tax on Salary Income

Residents: Monthly salary is withheld according to the “Salary Income Withholding Tax Table” by bracket (factoring in the exemption amount + standard deduction + salary special deduction)
Non-residents (less than 183 days of residence): Total monthly salary × 18% (from 2024; when exceeding the NT$39,600 basic threshold)
Progressive Income Tax Rates: 5% (≤NT$560,000) / 12% (up to NT$1,260,000) / 20% (up to NT$2,520,000) / 30% (up to NT$4,720,000) / 40%
Employees file their own consolidated income tax return in May of the following year; employer monthly withholding is a prepayment in nature
Labour Pension voluntary contribution 6% tax exemption: Employee voluntary contributions are deducted from the current year’s income, reducing the tax burden

MARCO INSIGHT

Taiwan’s salary withholding tax table has already internalized the exemption amount (NT$92,000/year), standard deduction (NT$124,000/year), and salary special deduction (NT$207,000/year). Employers can determine the withholding amount simply by referencing the table, without calculating each item individually. However, foreign employees are subject to a fixed 18% withholding rate, and may apply for a refund based on resident tax rates after residing for 183 days or more.

MARCO INSIGHT

Taiwan’s employer supplementary NHI premium involves two layers of calculation: first, the individual supplementary premium when “a single bonus exceeds 4 times the monthly insured amount” (2.11% withheld from the employee’s bonus); second, the employer supplementary premium on “the difference between total annual salary and total annual insured amount” (2.11% borne by the employer). The former is withheld monthly, while the latter is settled annually. Payroll must track both layers of logic simultaneously.
