Types of Work Visas

Saudi Arabia began reforming the sponsorship system (Kafala) through the Labour Reforms Initiative in 2021 and formally abolished the Kafala system in June 2025, shifting to contract-based employment relationships. In Q1 2026, Saudi Arabia introduced a 5-year physical Iqama, a skills-based work permit classification system, and new Premium Residency categories. The main work-related visa / residence permit types are:

Visa / Residence Type Who It Applies To Description (2026)
Work Visa + Iqama Regular foreign employees sponsored by an employer The employer first applies for Block Visa quota through Qiwa. The employee enters with an Entry Visa and converts to Iqama (residence card) within 90 days. Valid for 1–2 years and renewable; from 2026, a 5-year physical Iqama card is introduced.
Skills-based Work Permit (new in 2026) All new / renewed work permit applicants Applicants are classified as High-Skilled / Skilled / Basic based on five dimensions: education, work experience, professional skills, wage level, and age. Different tiers receive different fees, dependent rights, and mobility rules.
RHQ Work Permit (Regional HQ) Employees of multinational companies with regional headquarters in Saudi Arabia Enjoys 10-year Saudization exemption, zero income tax, and accelerated visa handling. Applicants are not counted under ordinary quota.
Premium Residency High-net-worth / high-skilled / special talent individuals No employer sponsorship required. Includes Permanent (SAR 4,000 — new 2026 permanent tier), Special Talent, Investor, Real Estate (property >= SAR 4 million), etc.
Visit / Business Visa Short-term business, meetings, visits 1–90 days. Cannot establish employment relationship, but since 2024 limited short-term activities such as business negotiations are allowed.
Dependent Visa Spouse / children of Iqama-holding employees Linked to the sponsor’s Iqama validity. Spouse may separately apply for a work permit. Female dependents aged 21+ may independently apply for residence.

The regular Work Visa path (Block Visa + Iqama) together with the 2026 skills classification is the mainstream route. The following focuses on this route, while also covering Premium Residency and RHQ exceptions.

Applicant Requirements

1. Employee-Side Requirements

  • Usually aged 21–60, with stricter limits for regulated professions such as healthcare and education.

  • Academic certificates must complete full chain attestation: notarization → home-country foreign ministry → Saudi embassy / consulate abroad → Saudi MOFA / cultural office.

  • Work experience must match the role. Some roles must be recognized under the Saudi Standard Classification of Occupations.

  • Regulated professions such as doctors, nurses, engineers, accountants, and lawyers must pass the relevant Saudi professional association certification, such as SCFHS, SCE, or SOCPA.

  • Medical examination must be completed at a GAMCA / GCCHMC-approved clinic in the home country, covering HIV, HBV, HCV, TB, etc.

  • Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) is required from the home country and any country of residence over 6 months, with full chain attestation.

2. Skills Classification (New in 2026)

MHRSD (Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development) assesses all work permits across five dimensions:

Dimension Assessment Notes
Education Score increases by level: high school / diploma / bachelor / master / doctorate.
Experience Years of experience relevant to the role.
Skills Certification and professional qualifications such as SCE / SCFHS, etc.
Wage Higher score for wages above industry average.
Age Age 21–35 receives the highest score; score decreases above age 50.

Skills Tier Impact

The combined score determines the tier (High-Skilled / Skilled / Basic). The tier affects Iqama fees, dependent accompaniment rights, and cross-employer mobility convenience.

3. Employer-Side Requirements

  • Holds valid Commercial Registration (CR) and Chamber Certificate.

  • Complies with Saudization (Nitaqat) quota. Companies are classified into Platinum / Green / Yellow / Red based on industry, company size, and Saudi employee ratio. Red-zone companies cannot apply for new work visas.

  • Registers employment contracts electronically on Qiwa. MUSANED is used for domestic work and certain sectors.

  • Applies first for Block Visa (approved foreign worker quota), then applies for a visa number for the specific employee.

  • RHQ companies certified by the Ministry of Investment of Saudi Arabia (MISA) enjoy 10-year Saudization exemption.

  • Since 2024, all employers must provide medical insurance for foreign employees (Class A or above) and register it in the CCHI system.

Important Reminder

Saudization (Nitaqat) is a hard employer-side requirement. If the company is classified in the Red zone, all new and renewed work permits are frozen, which directly affects employee Iqama status. Before accepting an offer, employees should understand the employer’s Nitaqat level to avoid residence-status risk.

Application Document Checklist

1. Employee Documents

1.

Valid passport with remaining validity >= 6 months and at least 2 blank pages.

2.

4 passport photos (4×6cm, white background).

3.

Academic certificates and transcripts: notarized in home country → authenticated by home-country foreign ministry → authenticated by Saudi embassy / consulate abroad → re-authenticated by Saudi MOFA after arrival.

4.

Work experience certificates covering the latest 5 years, also requiring full chain attestation.

5.

Professional association certification for regulated professions: SCFHS (medical), SCE (engineering), SOCPA (accounting), etc.

6.

Employment contract issued by the Saudi employer (Arabic + English bilingual), uploaded to Qiwa.

7.

Medical examination report from a GAMCA / GCCHMC-approved clinic in the home country (HIV, HBV, HCV, TB, ECG, etc.).

8.

Police Clearance Certificate from the home country + recent country of residence, with full chain attestation.

9.

Marriage certificate / birth certificate if dependents accompany, with full chain attestation.

10.

Enjaz online visa application form (visa.mofa.gov.sa) + visa number.

2. Employer Documents

1.

Valid Commercial Registration (CR) copy.

2.

Chamber of Commerce certification.

3.

Nitaqat compliance proof (Saudization level Green or above).

4.

Block Visa quota approved by MHRSD (foreign employee quota).

5.

Electronic visa number obtained through Absher Business / Qiwa (Enjaz visa number).

6.

MOFA invitation letter issued through visa.mofa.gov.sa.

7.

Employment contract (Arabic primary, English secondary) + Qiwa electronic contract registration.

8.

Employee medical insurance policy registered in the CCHI system.

Note — Chain Attestation

“Chain Attestation” is the biggest pain point for Saudi work visas. All non-Arabic / English originals must be notarized in the home country, authenticated by the home-country foreign ministry, authenticated by the Saudi embassy / consulate, and then re-authenticated by Saudi MOFA after arrival. The whole chain usually takes 4–8 weeks, so it should start immediately after signing the offer. Academic credentials can be checked through the “Saudi Verify” platform to confirm whether they are recognized by Saudi education authorities.

Application Process

Process Overview

Step Action Responsible Party Estimated Time
1 Employer applies for Block Visa through MHRSD (approved foreign worker quota). Employer 2–4 weeks
2 Employer applies for employee visa number through visa.mofa.gov.sa / Absher Business and issues MOFA invitation letter. Employer 1–2 weeks
3 Employee completes full chain attestation + GAMCA medical exam + PCC. Employee 4–8 weeks
4 Employee submits application and passport stamping at Saudi embassy / visa center (Enjaz) in home country. Employee 1–3 weeks
5 Employee enters Saudi Arabia with Entry Visa. Employee Within visa validity (usually 90 days)
6 Within 90 days after entry: employer handles biometrics, Iqama registration, medical insurance (CCHI), Muqeem / Absher registration. Employer 30–90 days
7 GOSI registration + Wage Protection System (WPS) salary-payment system enrollment. Employer Within 30 days after entry
8 Receive Iqama card (from 2026, 5-year physical chip card) + register Tawakkalna / Absher apps. Employee 1–2 weeks after Iqama registration

Detailed Steps

1.

Employer applies for Block Visa on the MHRSD platform. The available number of foreign employees is allocated based on Saudization level and job type.

2.

After Block Visa approval, employer submits employee information in Absher Business to generate the visa number and issues the MOFA invitation letter through visa.mofa.gov.sa, including the visa number.

3.

Employee uses the visa number to book and submit documents through the home-country Enjaz visa center, the official service point for visa.mofa.gov.sa.

4.

Embassy / visa center reviews and stamps the D-type work Entry Visa, usually taking 1–3 weeks.

5.

Employee enters Saudi Arabia with Entry Visa and must complete Iqama registration within 90 days: medical check + biometrics + fingerprinting in Jawazat / Absher system.

6.

Employer uploads the electronic contract on Qiwa and registers the employee bank account for wage payment through Mudad / WPS.

7.

Iqama card issuance: from 2026, a 5-year physical chip card replaces the previous 1–2 year card. Employee can check Iqama status and validity immediately through the Absher app.

Note — Core E-Government Platforms

Qiwa (HR platform), Absher (citizen services), Muqeem (foreign employee management), Tawakkalna (health code / government services), and Musaned (domestic work) are Saudi Arabia’s five core e-government platforms. Employers must activate an Absher Business account; employees must activate a personal Absher account after entry. Regulated professions must first pass association certification, such as SCFHS medical checks + exams, which may take 1–3 months, so it is best to start before arrival.

Arrival Guide

Important Reminder

Entry Visa holders may legally work after entering Saudi Arabia, but Iqama registration must be completed within 90 days. Otherwise, daily fines of SAR 200–1,000 may apply and deportation may be triggered. The Iqama card is the only universal identity document in Saudi Arabia; without it, almost nothing can be processed.

1. Work-Related

1.

Within 90 days after entry: employer arranges biometrics and medical check (public hospital appointment through CCHI / Mawid system), then completes Iqama registration.

2.

Receive Iqama card (5-year physical chip card from 2026) and activate personal Absher account.

3.

GOSI (General Organization for Social Insurance) registration: employee contributes 10% of wage (pension 9% + unemployment support 1%); employer contributes 12%.

4.

Enroll in Wage Protection System (WPS): all salaries must be paid through a Saudi bank account and employer submits monthly through Mudad.

5.

Regulated professions: complete transfer to local registration with SCFHS / SCE / SOCPA and similar associations.

6.

Register Tawakkalna app (health code / government services) + Muqeem platform (Iqama / entry-exit / renewal status checking).

2. Daily Life

1.

Open a bank account: Al Rajhi Bank, Saudi National Bank (SNB), Riyad Bank, Alinma, etc.; Iqama + employment contract + GOSI proof required.

2.

Housing: lease through Ejar, the housing contract registration system. Contracts must be registered on Ejar. Common platforms include Aqar, Bayut, and Hectarapp.

3.

SIM card: STC, Mobily, Zain; real-name registration with Iqama; foreign users may hold up to 10 SIM cards per person.

4.

Transport: Riyadh Metro opened in 2024; Uber / Careem are widely available. A Saudi driving license can be applied for through Absher using a home-country license; some countries are exempt from road test.

5.

Mobile payment: mada national payment network + Apple Pay / STC Pay / Tabby / Tamara are highly common. Mada card is the local standard.

6.

Healthcare: employer must provide CCHI-certified private medical insurance (Class A minimum). Foreign employees cannot use Saudi citizen healthcare except emergency care.

3. Dependent Visas

  • Dependent Visa: after receiving Iqama, the employee may sponsor spouse / minor children (male < 18, female no age limit) + unmarried financially dependent parents.

  • Dependent Iqama validity follows the employee. After the 2025 reform, spouse may independently apply for a work permit without relying on the employee.

  • Dependents must complete GAMCA medical check + CCHI insurance; spouse may independently apply for a Saudi driving license.

  • Schooling: public schools mainly use Arabic Saudi national curriculum; foreign families usually choose international schools such as BSR, KAS, ASG, etc.

  • Premium Residency holders do not require a sponsor and may sponsor dependents independently.

Existing Iqama: Changing Employers

Saudi Arabia formally abolished the Kafala sponsorship system in June 2025. The relationship between foreign employees and employers is now contract-based. Employees may change employers without original employer consent when conditions are met, and the transfer is handled through the Qiwa platform. Iqama no longer “belongs” to the employer; it is tied to the individual and a valid contract.

1. Core Rules

Item 2026 Current Rule
Relationship between Iqama and employer Iqama is tied to the individual rather than the employer. Contract changes are synchronized through Qiwa.
Is original employer consent required? Generally yes; however, consent is not required if any exemption condition is met.
Number of employer changes No limit, but each transfer must be registered through Qiwa.
Minimum contract service period Usually 1 year. Changing employer within the first year may require compensation or an exemption condition.
Does Iqama need a new application? No new application. After the new employer completes Tanazul (sponsorship transfer) in Qiwa, Iqama is automatically updated.
Fees New employer bears transfer fee (around SAR 2,000) and new work permit fees.

2. Exemption Conditions Allowing Direct Transfer Without Original Employer Consent

1.

Iqama has expired for more than 3 days.

2.

Employer failed to issue a work permit within 3 months after employee entry.

3.

Salary was not paid for more than 3 consecutive months; WPS records can be used as evidence.

4.

Employer is classified in the Nitaqat “Red” zone.

5.

Employer company is cancelled / Commercial Registration is revoked.

6.

Employment contract expires and is not renewed.

3. Practical Steps to Change Employers

1.

New employer sends an electronic Job Offer / transfer request (Tanazul) through Qiwa.

2.

Employee reviews and accepts the Offer in the personal Qiwa account.

3.

Original employer receives Qiwa notification. If exemption applies, the system automatically approves the transfer and the original employer cannot reject it; otherwise a 60-day notice period begins.

4.

After the notice period ends / automatic approval is granted, the new employer pays the transfer fee + new work permit fee.

5.

Iqama information updates automatically in Absher / Muqeem. Employee keeps the original card; no card replacement is required.

6.

GOSI, CCHI, and WPS links are updated by the new employer.

4. Old Kafala vs New Contract-Based System

Item Kafala Era (<= 2024) 2026 New Contract System
Employer change authorization Original employer’s written consent (Tanazul) was mandatory. Consent not required if exemption conditions are met.
Exit Re-entry authorization Controlled by employer. Employee can self-process through Absher (opened).
Final Exit Initiated exclusively by employer. Employee may self-process when contract ends / legal grounds exist.
Wage protection Dependent on employer. WPS mandatory bank payment + Mudad supervision.
Cross-city work Employer-limited location. Qiwa contract states location; freely negotiable.
Dispute resolution Dependent on employer / administration. Electronic labour-court litigation through Najiz.

Important Reminder

Although Kafala has been abolished, non-exempt transfers during the first contract term (usually 1 year) under the new contract system may still create: 1) financial compensation depending on contract terms; 2) loss or reduction of End of Service Benefit (ESB) on a pro-rated basis. Before signing, clarify probation period, minimum service period, and penalty clauses. If high ESB is involved, keep WPS records and resignation / termination letters as evidence.

5. Premium Residency and Employer Change

Foreign nationals holding Premium Residency are not subject to employer sponsorship restrictions. They may change employers freely, work as self-employed, start a business, or remain unemployed, and may independently sponsor dependents. From 2026, Saudi Arabia introduced a SAR 4,000 Permanent Residency Scheme, fully outside the ordinary Iqama system.

Departure Guide

1.

Submit resignation / contract termination notice according to the employment contract, usually 30–60 days. Employer must register “Contract Termination” in Qiwa.

2.

Settle End of Service Benefit (ESB / Mukafaa): under Saudi Labour Law Article 84, first 5 years = 0.5 month basic wage per year; after 5 years = 1 month basic wage per year. Full amount applies for dismissal / contract expiry; voluntary resignation is tiered by service length (< 2 years: none; 2–5 years: 1/3; 5–10 years: 2/3; >= 10 years: full).

3.

Settle remaining annual leave (>= 21 days per year), 13th salary / overtime, and other payable items.

4.

Employer cancels work permit and applies for Final Exit Visa through Absher / Jawazat. Employee must ensure Iqama has at least 30 days remaining validity.

5.

Cancel medical insurance (CCHI) + GOSI registration. Contribution records remain in the system.

6.

Close bank account: keep it for 1–2 months to settle final WPS salary and utility bills. Close STC Pay / mada and other local payment services.

7.

Cancel phone / internet / electricity contracts; return company car / apartment keys; terminate lease in the Ejar system.

8.

GOSI withdrawal: only Saudi citizens have pension. Non-GCC foreign employees generally do not receive refund of individual pension contributions. Some countries have bilateral social security agreements with Saudi Arabia, such as the UK, allowing contribution periods to be counted in the home system.

9.

Leave Saudi Arabia within 60 days after Final Exit. Re-entry under the original employer is not allowed after Final Exit. If returning to work in Saudi Arabia, a new visa application is required. The automatic 3-year re-entry ban was cancelled in 2024.

Key Policy Changes from 2021 to 2026

As a core part of Vision 2030, Saudi Arabia has gradually advanced labour and immigration reforms since 2021. The goal is to attract global high-end talent and modernize the labour market before 2030. Key changes include:

Change Item Details
Labour Reforms Initiative (2021) For the first time, allowed employer change, exit, and final exit without original employer consent under specific conditions (Tanazul / Exit / Final Exit self-service).
Kafala System Abolished (2025.6) Sponsorship system officially abolished. Foreign employees and employers shifted to contract-based relationships; Iqama no longer “belongs” to the employer.
Skills-Based Work Permit (2026 Q1) Classifies work permits into High-Skilled / Skilled / Basic based on education, experience, skills, wage, and age; each tier has different fees and rights.
5-Year Physical Iqama (2026 Q1) Iqama upgraded to 5-year physical chip card (previously 1–2 years); instant verification through Absher.
Premium Residency Expanded Added Special Talent, Gifted, Investor, Real Estate (property >= SAR 4 million) categories + SAR 4,000 permanent residency tier newly introduced in 2026.
RHQ Program (from 2024) Multinational companies establishing regional headquarters in Saudi Arabia enjoy 10-year Saudization exemption + 30-year zero income tax. Since 2024, government procurement is limited to companies with RHQ established.
WPS Full-Sector Coverage (2025) Wage Protection System covers companies of all sizes. Mudad supervises all salary payments. 3 months of unpaid salary triggers Qiwa transfer exemption.
Emergency Visa Extension (2026 Q1) Employers may apply online for up to 3 months of work visa / Iqama extension.
3-Year Re-Entry Ban Cancelled (2024) Exit Re-entry overstay no longer automatically triggers a 3-year ban; replaced by manual review.
Independent Sponsorship for Female Foreign Employees (2025) Female foreign employees aged 21+ may independently sponsor dependents and process Iqama, no longer relying on a male sponsor.
