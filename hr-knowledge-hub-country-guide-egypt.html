Salary Benchmark

Statutory minimum wage (from March 2025)

Private-sector minimum monthly wage: EGP 7,000 (~USD 140) – doubled since January 2024.
Public sector: will increase to EGP 8,000 / month from July 2026.
Part-time minimum hourly wage: EGP 28 (net) – mandatory annual increase ≥ 3% of social insurance wage or EGP 250 (whichever is higher).

Mandatory benefits / common benefits

Profit sharing: employer must distribute 10% of annual net profit to employees (cap = employee annual salary).
Mandatory annual increase: ≥ 3% of social insurance wage or EGP 250 / month (whichever is higher).
Sick leave: employer pays 75% for the first 3 months; then social insurance fund pays (up to 12 months).
Maternity leave: 120 days paid (New Labour Law 14/2025); up to 3 times in lifetime.

Contracts must be written (Arabic); New Labour Law 14/2025 takes effect on 1 September 2025; electronic contracts are valid.

Dismissal protection and severance (New Labour Law 14/2025)

Dismissal requires a final judgment from the specialised labour court (operational from October 2025).
Severance pay: 1 month salary for each completed year of service (new law raises the standard).
Resignation notice period: 3 months (increased from 2 months); resignation may be withdrawn within 10 days.

Key Regulations

New Labour Law No. 14/2025 (effective September 2025)

Replaces old Law 12/2003; adds remote work / job sharing / digital contracts; maternity leave increases to 120 days; resignation notice period becomes 3 months.

Social Insurance Law No. 148/2019

Unified social insurance system; social insurance lower / upper limits increase by +15% annually (until 2027); employer 18.75% + employee 11%; applies equally to foreign employees.

Profit Sharing

Employers must distribute 10% of annual net profit to employees (cap = employee annual salary); minimum 1 month salary.

Specialised Labour Courts (from October 2025)

Dismissal requires a final judgment from the labour court (decision within 3 months); significantly strengthens employee dismissal protection.

Foreign Employee Hiring Rules

Foreign employees must not exceed 10% of headcount / 20% of total payroll; work permit from the Ministry of Labour is required.
