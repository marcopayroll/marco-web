Scope & Application

Scope & Legal Basis / Estatuto de los Trabajadores · Ley General SS · Ley IRPF

This document applies to:

Companies that have established a local legal entity in Spain (Sociedad Limitada SL minimum capital €3,000 / Sociedad Anónima SA minimum €60,000)
Employees directly hired by the local entity, subject to the Estatuto de los Trabajadores (Workers’ Statute) and the applicable Convenio Colectivo (collective agreement)
Payroll compliance is based on ET, the Ley General SS (LGSS), the Ley IRPF, and AEAT / TGSS regulations

This framework explains how companies complete Payroll Set-up in Spain (AEAT tax registration, TGSS employer registration, NIE / NIF employee identification), monthly payroll calculation (IRPF withholding + Seguridad Social contributions from both parties), monthly Sistema RED social security filing, and Modelo 111 / 190 PIT filing.

2026 KEY NUMBERS

Salario Mínimo (SMI) €1,221/month × 14 pagas = €17,094/year
IRPF Tax Brackets 19% – 47% 6 tiers + regional surcharge
Employee SS Rate 6.48% Contingencias + MEI
Employer SS Rate 30.57% + FOGASA + training fee
SS Contribution Ceiling €5,101.20 Monthly ceiling (€61,214/year)
Annual Leave 23 days Convenio typically 30 calendar days
Beckham Law 24% flat rate ≤ €600k · 6 years
Maternity / Paternity 16 weeks Each parent; first 6 weeks mandatory

MARCO INSIGHT

Three key characteristics of Spanish Payroll: (1) The 14 pagas system — beyond the base monthly salary, an extra payment (paga extraordinaria) is issued each in June and December; an employee’s total annual salary = monthly salary × 14; (2) Convenio Colectivo sector collective agreement is an implicit mandate — minimum wage, annual leave days, overtime rate, and allowances are all set out in detail by it; (3) Employer social security of 30%+ has no monthly fluctuation and is fixed every month (capped by the €5,101.20 ceiling).

MARCO INSIGHT

Three commonly underestimated details in Spanish Payroll: (1) The monthly contribution base ceiling of €5,101.20 (€61,214/year) applies to Social Security, not PIT — above this, employer Social Security contributions stop increasing but IRPF progression continues; (2) The MEI intergenerational mechanism enters its 4th phase in 2026 (employee 0.13% + employer 0.67%), rising to 1.2% by 2050; (3) Convenio Colectivo sector agreements can substantially raise standards — e.g. banking sector annual leave of 30+ working days / Christmas bonus, etc. — once signed, the employer must pay the extra amounts.

MARCO INSIGHT

Three key changes to Spanish Payroll in 2026: (1) The SMI minimum wage increased 3.1% to €1,221/month × 14 pagas = €17,094/year; IRPF parameters were adjusted in tandem to ensure the minimum wage remains fully exempt from PIT; (2) The MEI intergenerational mechanism enters its 4th phase (employee 0.13% + employer 0.67%), with further annual increases ahead; (3) Beckham Law eligibility was expanded after the 2023 reform to cover remote workers, professional managers, and R&D workers, increasing tax planning scope for non-EU cross-border employees.
